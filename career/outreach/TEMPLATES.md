# Outreach Templates — Miles Tipton

Rules that apply to all of these: send the same day you apply, keep it under 120 words, one ask, no attachments in a first LinkedIn message, and never open with "I hope this finds you well." Replace every `[BRACKET]` — a template that still has brackets in it gets deleted unread.

---

## 1. Cover letter (use only when required or when there's a real hook)

> Dear [Hiring Manager name — find it; "Hiring Team" if you truly can't],
>
> I'm applying for the [ROLE] position at [COMPANY]. I've spent three years on both sides of technical sales: I ran pre-sales demos of Generative AI and Conversational AI products to 20+ executives at Inbenta, and I closed $60,000 in managed IT and professional services contracts in three months at Homegrown Solutions.
>
> What I think separates me for this role is that I build what I sell. At UPath.ai I architected multi-agent AI workflows on OpenAI AgentKit and n8n that cut prospect research time by 40%, and I built a custom CRM that pulls and enriches lead data through APIs. When a prospect asks how the integration actually works, I've done it.
>
> [ONE SENTENCE, SPECIFIC TO THE COMPANY — a product observation, a competitor you sold against, something from their engineering blog. If you can't write this sentence honestly, this company isn't worth an application today.]
>
> I'd welcome the chance to talk about how I'd contribute to [TEAM].
>
> Miles Tipton

> Hi [NAME] — last note from me on [ROLE]. If the timing isn't right or the role's filled, no problem at all — I'd just ask that you keep me in mind for solutions or sales engineering openings down the line. Thanks for your time.

## 8. Post-interview thank-you (send within 4 hours, every time)

> Hi [NAME] — thanks for the time today. [SPECIFIC THING FROM THE CONVERSATION.]
>
> One thing I want to add on [QUESTION YOU ANSWERED WEAKLY]: [BETTER ANSWER, 2-3 SENTENCES].
>
> [IF A TECHNICAL QUESTION CAME UP: I put together a quick example of the workflow we discussed — link here.]
>
> Looking forward to next steps.

The middle paragraph is the one that matters. Every candidate sends a thank-you; almost none use it to repair their weakest answer. Do that and you'll move up the stack.
