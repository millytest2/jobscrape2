Positioning

Stop applying as an SDR

Your five-variant set was five SDR/BDR resumes differing only in the summary line and the skills row — the work experience underneath, which is what carries the keyword score, was byte-identical. That's the wrong tier and cosmetic differentiation at the same time.

SDR is the most commoditized role in tech sales. You'd be competing against 22-year-olds at entry pay while holding a real Sales Engineer title and a $60K close. You'd be bidding against your own resume.

Your actual position: you sell technical products and you build with the technology you sell. Forward-deployed and solutions-consultant postings at AI companies rose roughly 4× through 2026, and every one of those job descriptions wants someone who can run discovery Tuesday and wire up an API integration Wednesday. Very few candidates do both. You have receipts for both.

Tables to break parsing

Three gates, in order. Most candidates optimize for the first and lose at the second.

  • The parser. All dates are now MM/YYYY — parsers reliably mis-read 2026 - Present. Standard section headers, no tables, no text boxes, no headers or footers.
  • Keyword and title matching. Title match dominates the score, so each variant carries a target-title line under your name. Skills blocks give both forms — Large Language Models (LLM), proof of concept (POC) — because different systems index different ones.
  • The human, at about seven seconds. Current title, employer, one number, coherent story. Your best number is now above the fold.

Don't use the white-text trick

Hidden keyword stuffing is the most-circulated “ATS hack” right now and it no longer works. Every ATS shows recruiters a plain-text view where hidden text appears in full, and several flag it automatically. Everything in these resumes is visible text you can defend out loud.

The plan

Ten a week, never cold

Board applications convert at roughly 2–3%. This is built to beat that and kept small enough that you'll actually do it: 10 applications, 15 recruiter or hiring-manager messages, 5 referral asks per week.

The rule that decides the outcome: never apply cold. For every application, find the hiring manager or a recruiter and message them the same day. Application plus message converts several times better than application alone.

  1. Pick the matching variant and open the job description.
  2. Run keyword_check.py against it. Add missing keywords only if they're genuinely true of you.
  3. Rename the file Miles_Tipton_<Company>_<Role>.docx — recruiters see filenames.
  4. Submit .docx unless the posting demands PDF. Workday and iCIMS parse Word more reliably.
  5. Message the hiring manager and one recruiter the same day.
  6. Log it, then follow up on day 4 and day 10. Almost nobody follows up; it's free advantage.

Sequence it

Don't fire at your top five dream companies in week one. Run 5–10 LA SaaS and sales-tech applications first, do those screens, and get your $60K story sharp under real pressure. Then go at the AI vendors. One shot per company per role — spend the early reps where a miss costs least.

Interviews

Three stories, rehearsed cold

You'll hit asynchronous AI video screens at a meaningful share of these companies. They score structure over charisma: answer in STAR, echo the job description's actual words, keep it 60–90 seconds, look at the lens rather than the preview window, and pause instead of saying “um.”

  • The $60K close. Discovery, what you found, how you scoped it, how you closed. Your best story — lead with it.
  • A demo that went sideways. A technical question you couldn't answer at Inbenta, what you did in the room, how you followed up. A clean recovery beats a flawless run.
  • Something you built. The AgentKit/n8n CRM end to end — problem, architecture, the 40%. This is what separates you from everyone else in the stack. Have the GitHub link ready.

And the question you'll get every single time

“Why so many roles in three years?” Don't get defensive: “I took contract and startup work deliberately to get range across the technical sales stack — pre-sales at Inbenta, closing at Homegrown, building the product at UPath. I'm looking for one place to go deep now.” One breath, then move on.

Do now

Three things before you send anything

  1. Verify every title and date against your offer letters, then make LinkedIn match exactly.
  2. Replace the LinkedIn placeholder in resume_data.json with your real profile URL, or delete the line. Not one of your five resumes had one — recruiters check before they reply.
  3. Open each .docx and confirm it's one page in your copy of Word.

Then, in priority order

  • Get the outcome numbers you're missing: quota attainment, average deal size, cycle length, win rate. One real attainment number outweighs every activity metric on the page.
  • Learn MEDDIC/MEDDPICC this week — it's in a large share of SE and AE job descriptions and it's a common first-screen question. Add it to your skills line only once you can walk through it.
  • Decide the Reyvisum question: real engagement with contract dates, or off the resume. No third option.
